Never-Skilling, a relatively new term in the age of Artificial Intelligence, simply means that you never learn a particular foundational skill because AI is there to do it for you. In the pre-AI days, if a person had to write an essay, they had to build up an original idea, structure their cases, uphold arguments, defend their points and most importantly make mistakes from which they could improve. But now, they can give a prompt to any LLM, and it will write down that essay within seconds. But this obviously goes beyond simple essays. Design may be going through one of its weakest phases in recent memory, as many designs nowadays are made with generative AI. AI generated designs on logos, posters, and banners are at their peak. A recently popularized term is "vibe coding", where people can give AI some prompts and it will design websites, apps or even simple software for them. This essentially makes learning the basics of a programming language obsolete.

How AI works is that the corporations behind these train them on actual human made content from throughout history. Books, articles, blogs and many more human creations are fed into these AI to train them, and these chatbots analyze and compile those materials when generating contents. As the training materials are almost the same across the LLMs, the content they churn out often shares the core features and patterns. This breeds unnatural conformity among the outputs, directly detectable to a human mind which is pretty good at detecting patterns. For example, a common difference between human writing and AI is the "stylistic tic" as AI is likely to overuse (-) em-dashes or use phrases like "delve into" more than the usual.

Imagine a child of a primary school is handed a calculator. Or a 7th grade student gets to use a scientific calculator in his exams. The idea of learning how to solve math problems manually becomes irrelevant for them. They can simply do these calculations on their calculators without knowing how the basic mathematical functions work. That's a classic never-skilling example. Another term we cannot ignore is "deskilling". If a person learnt those calculations, but after relying on calculators for ages forgets the basics, that's deskilling. In the context of AI, it's equally as dangerous as never-skilling. Too much dependency on AI and even those who learn the basics, at some point start losing their natural skills. A counter argument here can be "calculators did not make people dumber; they just changed how people did math." But the difference here is that calculators automate a very specific task which always provides a verifiable right answer. But, writing, designs, coding etc. involve judgement, originality and error-correcting that simply do not have one quantifiable correct output.

If this vicious phenomenon of never-skilling and deskilling is not controlled, people will start to lose the incentives to learn new skills as AI is there to do it for them. A large portion of the population will outsource their basic natural abilities to think, create, and imagine for themselves, to some Large Language Models which are already notorious for their outputs that are mediocre, unnatural and too similar across the board.
