## 2. What SICDP works on

Two focus areas:

1. **Climate governance.** How plans for salinity, riverbank erosion, drought and floods hold up where people live with them.
2. **Digital governance and state capacity.** Whether public institutions are ready for the technology they are asked to run.

### Programmes (approved wording, use as written)

**The C30 Agenda** (field research; "C30" is SICDP's own name, from "climate beyond 2030")
> The C30 Agenda is SICDP's first major field research project. It audits where climate-related Sustainable Development Goal targets failed to deliver for climate-vulnerable communities and proposes climate priorities for the decade beyond 2030, grounded in direct field evidence from Bangladesh. Research teams work across four sites, each representing a distinct hazard: coastal salinity, riverine erosion, drought, and haor flash floods. Findings are qualitative-led, drawn from interviews and focus groups.

**The Adaptation Lab** (applied research)
> The Adaptation Lab is the applied research arm of SICDP. It takes a single live case at a time and moves it through a structured cycle: intake, active case work, after-action review, and a published dispatch. A dual-wing design also supports an innovation and prototyping track. Each case must name a clear beneficiary, and the Lab documents what it learns so that practical adaptation lessons are shared openly with practitioners and policymakers.

Describe the Lab only in these general terms. Do not name any specific case or project.

**The Statecraft Papers** (publication series): SICDP's working paper series. **Not yet launched; always label it "Forthcoming".** Papers will be published openly on the website, on SSRN and on Zenodo. Its standard: every paper should be useful to someone making a decision.

### Plans (always label as "Coming up" or "Forthcoming", never as done)

- Work with the Rural Development Academy (RDA), Bogura. A proposal is in process. Say only "Coming up"; give no dates or programme details until it is approved.
- Publish working papers openly (website, SSRN, Zenodo).
- Build long-term partnerships with research institutions in Bangladesh and South Asia.

### Real engagement so far

## 5. Voice and style

- **Plain, specific, calm.** Write like a careful researcher talking to an intelligent friend. Short sentences. Concrete nouns: district names, dates, real numbers.
- **Honest about stage.** SICDP is new. Never inflate. "Forthcoming", "Coming up" and "being formed" are fine and expected.
- **Non-partisan.** No party names, slogans, leaders' images or political symbols. Criticise policies and institutions with evidence, never people or parties.
- **Rooted, not exotic.** Bangladesh is home, not a backdrop. Avoid poverty clichés and pity framing; show agency and expertise of local people.
- **English** for the website and formal outputs. Bengali for community-facing and social media content when useful.
- **Never use em dashes** in SICDP copy. Use commas, colons or a new sentence.
- Avoid AI-sounding filler: "delve", "in today's rapidly evolving world", "empowering communities", "unlock the potential", "not just X but Y", lists of three adjectives, rhetorical questions as openers.
- Dates: "26 October 2026". Places: "Sariakandi, Bogura". Author dates on articles show the real month or day only; never invent a date or time.

- Unifying motif: the **nakshi kantha running stitch** (dashed lines like hand stitching): frames, dividers, a faint weave in the background.
- Illustration style: clean single-weight line art, lots of white space, maximum two motifs per design.
- Font on the website: Figtree (free Google Font). Logotype: condensed caps (Gobold) in the brand files.
- Avoid in images: party symbols, paddy sheaf, canopy boat as a central symbol, flags, stock photos of handshakes or laptops, generated faces.

## 6. Hard rules (apply to every piece of content)

1. Never invent content: no fake publications, numbers, quotes, testimonials, partners, advisers or "confirmed" events.
2. Nobody appears as team, adviser or partner without written consent.
3. Anything not done yet is labelled "Forthcoming" or "Coming up".
4. Non-partisan in words and images.
5. Cite sources for facts and figures; if a source is missing, say so or leave the claim out.
6. Op-eds and articles carry: "Views are the author's own and do not necessarily reflect those of SICDP." Shihab approves them before publication.
7. Respect consent and privacy in field content: no private addresses, no GPS data, no one filmed without consent.
