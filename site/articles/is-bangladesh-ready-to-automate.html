Is Bangladesh Ready to Automate?

Md. Imam Hossain Shihab · Written July 2026

You are a biker rushing through the roads of Dhaka, the slowest city in the world according to a US NBER report, and you see the traffic light turn red the moment you are about to cross the road. What would you do? An average driver in Dhaka would not even care to look at the traffic lights. But that was before the seventh of May, because from that day on the Dhaka Metropolitan Police (DMP) decided to announce, "Not anymore!"

The traffic division of the DMP placed 25 AI-based cameras, integrated with 'Road Transport Act-2018 Violation Detection Software', at major intersections between Intercontinental and Jahangir Gate. The idea was to replace the manual traffic prosecution system with a digital one.

The manual system had noticeable flaws. It required stopping vehicles, causing traffic congestion and disrupting traffic flow. The paper-based slips and on-spot fines also consumed time, and the handwriting on the paper slips sometimes looked like a medical prescription.

The times are changing now. Hanan Rahman Jibon, a motorist, told Reuters that he received an automated text to pay a 2,000 taka fine after running a red light. The AI-enabled cameras can detect five types of traffic violations and they have recorded 300 cases within the first week of deployment.

The traffic police verify the footage before a case is formally issued. After verification, an SMS is sent to the offender, and the official notice is delivered to his or her home address by post. Offenders are given 10 to 15 days to pay the fine after receiving the notification.

On paper, the system is not just working, but it is something the Dhaka traffic has demanded for decades.

Let's ask ourselves the bigger question now. Will the system sustain itself in the long term?

There are reports of traffic violations regardless of the AI traffic police watching. A recent social media post revealed a person who was wrongly accused and received an automated SMS demanding fines for breaking traffic rules when he didn't do so. And one of the major headaches of the Dhaka streets, the auto-rickshaws known as Tesla, are still unregulated.

AI will make mistakes, but who is responsible when it does? You? The DMP? The AI? Does the DMP have the technical capacity to regularly update information in its database?

Talking about databases, we can look into the two NID data leak cases that happened last year. The personal data of 120 million people is stored in the NID database and 186 organizations now receive NID verification from the database.

On 10 February 2025, five institutions were found to have leaked database information to third parties. On May 7 of the same year, the Election Commission found two organizations actively leaking data and suspended their access to NID verification services.

This is the same quality database, without any improved regulation, protection or management, upon which we are deploying AI-enabled services.

Mark-Alexandre Doumba, the minister of digital economy and innovation of Gabon has coined the term 'Premature Automation,' which refers to the premature deployment of AI systems on top of fragile institutional foundations. He argued that, "If economies with weak fundamentals try to leapfrog into AI-enabled automation, the effort will only amplify their dysfunction."

Bangladesh has been busy lately, drafting the National AI Policy 2026-2030. The policy promises the future on the one hand: National Bangla LLM, AI Innovation Fund, Independent Oversight Committee and the formation of a permanent Artificial Intelligence Act from 2028, and on the other hand, it visibly contradicts itself.

The policy declares that 'Bangladesh can leapfrog into a leadership position in South Asia's AI landscape' and admits the capacity gaps in the same paragraph.

The word 'leapfrogging' can be used confidently today because of the previous digitalization success. But the context today is different. Mobile banking worked because the technology itself was the service. No bank branch needed to exist first.

AI deployment relies on the institutional foundations, it does not bypass them. It also inherits the existing flaws, data gaps and security loopholes of the system. An algorithm which issues traffic fines is as reliable as the database it pulls from.

The solution is not Anti-AI, but fulfilling a precondition before automating. The precondition is to achieve institutional maturity. We can safely deploy AI-enabled services on top of institutions that are mature and ready and rely on them.

At 25 intersections in Dhaka, the AI-enabled CCTV cameras are watching, soon to be expanded. The auto-rickshaws are still out of the frame. The law that will govern the service won't begin taking shape until 2028. Hanan Rahman Jibon paid his fine, but the same database that identified him leaked NID card information the year before.

It may look like the future has arrived in Dhaka on the seventh of May. But the institutional infrastructure that the future requires hasn't arrived yet.
