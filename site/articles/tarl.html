TaRL: Small Solution to a Big Problem

Shuaib Ahmed Araf · Written September 2026

The Bangladeshi Education System, according to most popular media, is faulty, ineffective, somewhat corrupt, and obviously needs change. And unfortunately this is true, and we cannot refine it instantly. What we can do is to specifically target each problem and bring changes there that have visible and measurable results. So, instead of reforming the whole system and holistically targeting all the problems at once through massive reforms, what we actually need to do is to find the most basic problem and solve it first.

Recently, the state minister of education visited a primary school and found out that a large number of students cannot properly read or write. This is a major issue in the grassroots education quality of government primary schools in Bangladesh. But instead of radical changes, it can be fixed using a simple mechanism known as TaRL (Teaching at the Right Level). TaRL is a system where the school evaluates each student's basic literacy capability, groups them into small cohorts based on the evaluation rather than age or grade, treats their shortcomings accordingly using a single class period every day, and runs along the regular curriculum. This is very effective in classrooms where students' educational understanding levels and learning speed differ significantly. TaRL was first initiated in India by Pratham and was later refined by J-PAL (MIT's Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab) through rigorous randomized evaluations.

The system looks something like this:

  • The school assesses the students' weaknesses with simple oral/written tests. For example, some students struggle to read or write, some might not be able to recognize words, some may struggle with simple mathematical functions, etc.
  • They essentially divide these students into small groups based on their specific needs.
  • These children are now pulled away from their regular classes for one period (45-60 minutes) and are put in a class that organizes them based on their learning levels.
  • Using games, flashcards, story cards, and group activities, they learn and bridge their gap.
  • These students are reassessed periodically and rearranged based on that.
  • This whole process runs alongside the regular classes that follow the regular grade-based curriculum.

The reason to choose this specific method is because it has been tested in India, Kenya, Zambia, Botswana, Nigeria, and Ghana, showing measurable positive impact on childhood learning and literacy abilities. It is essentially a catch-up mechanism rather than a replacement reform. So it's easy to execute, yet the effectiveness is high. This needs no new textbooks, no new hires, and only a very low extra cost. The materials can be designed by existing teachers as well as arranging the classes, just with one restructured period. Diagnosis is also very easy and straightforward. This makes this reform unusually feasible for execution, and instead of NGOs, it should be the government carrying this out.

The government can mandate that every school has this program, train the teachers to get used to this, and establish a centralized team to periodically check whether this system is running effectively or not.

Solving issues starts at the ground zero. Then move upwards. TaRL is a low-barrier, low-cost, effective solution to one of the most fundamental issues of primary education in Bangladesh. Institutionalizing and implementing it is the most cost-efficient educational reform initiative the government can take as it runs along what already exists. So if there is one reform initiative I want our government to undertake, it would be TaRL.
